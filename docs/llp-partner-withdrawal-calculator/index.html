The four withdrawal routes in an LLP

Share of profit — your slice of LLP profit per the LLP agreement Exempt u/s 10(2A)
Remuneration / salary — for working partners, must be authorised & quantified in the LLP agreement Taxable (slab) · deductible for LLP
Interest on capital — on capital contributed, deductible up to 12% p.a. Taxable (slab) · deductible for LLP
Drawings / capital withdrawal — taking back your own capital balance Not income — your own money
Why the LLP usually wins: a company pays corporate tax and then dividends are taxed again in the shareholder's hands (double taxation). In an LLP, once the profit is taxed at the LLP level, the profit share is tax-free when distributed — and remuneration + interest are deductible, shrinking the LLP's taxable base. There is no DDH / dividend tax.

Assumptions & disclaimer

This is a planning model, not tax advice. Validate every figure with your CA before acting.

  • LLP tax rate: 30% + 12% surcharge (if total income > ₹1 cr) + 4% cess.
  • Section 40(b) remuneration cap (FY 2024-25 onwards, per Finance Act 2024): on the first ₹6,00,000 of book profit — higher of ₹3,00,000 or 90%; on the balance — 60%. Confirm current-year limits.
  • "Book profit" for 40(b) = profit before remuneration but after deducting partner interest.
  • Interest on capital deductible only up to 12% p.a. and only if authorised in the LLP agreement.
  • Profit share is exempt in partners' hands u/s 10(2A) (already taxed in the LLP).
  • Remuneration & interest are taxed at the partners' marginal slab — set above; real tax depends on each partner's total income.
  • The Pvt Ltd comparison assumes identical salary drawn, residual profit distributed as dividend, dividend taxed at the same marginal rate. It ignores MAT/AMT, cess nuances, TDS timing, and state-specific items.
  • Tax-neutral conversion (Sec 47(xiiib)) conditions are separate and strict — turnover/asset thresholds, 50% profit-share continuity for 5 years, no payout from accumulated profits for 3 years. Breach → capital-gains tax on conversion. Not modelled here.
