The four withdrawal routes in an LLP

Share of profit — each partner's 50% slice per the LLP agreement Exempt u/s 10(2A)
Remuneration / salary — for working partners, authorised & quantified in the LLP agreement Taxable (slab) · deductible for LLP
Interest on capital — on capital contributed, deductible up to 12% p.a. Taxable (slab) · deductible for LLP
Drawings / capital withdrawal — taking back your own capital balance Not income — your own money
Why the LLP usually wins: a company pays corporate tax and then dividends are taxed again in each shareholder's hands (double taxation). In an LLP, once profit is taxed at the firm level, the profit share is tax-free when distributed — and remuneration + interest are deductible, shrinking the LLP's taxable base. No dividend tax.

Assumptions & disclaimer

Planning model, not tax advice. Validate every figure with your CA before acting.

  • Two partners, equal 50:50 profit-sharing ratio; both treated as working partners.
  • LLP tax: 30% + 12% surcharge (if income > ₹1 cr) + 4% cess.
  • Section 40(b) cap (FY 2024-25 onwards): on first ₹6,00,000 of book profit — higher of ₹3,00,000 or 90%; on the balance — 60%. Firm-level cap; total remuneration split 50:50.
  • "Book profit" for 40(b) = profit before remuneration but after partner interest.
  • Interest on capital deductible only up to 12% p.a. and only if authorised in the LLP agreement.
  • Profit share exempt in partners' hands u/s 10(2A).
  • Remuneration & interest taxed at each partner's marginal slab (set above).
  • Pvt Ltd comparison: identical salary drawn, residual profit distributed as dividend 50:50, dividend taxed at each shareholder's rate. Ignores MAT/AMT, TDS timing, state items.
  • Tax-neutral conversion (Sec 47(xiiib)) is a separate strict test (turnover/asset thresholds, 50% profit-share continuity for 5 years, no payout from accumulated profits for 3 years). Breach → capital-gains tax on conversion. Not modelled here.
