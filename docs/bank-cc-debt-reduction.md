# Reducing a ₹50 Crore Bank Cash Credit (CC) Balance

A Cash Credit facility is a working-capital line secured against current assets
(inventory + receivables) up to a sanctioned "drawing power". A ₹50 cr outstanding
balance is not "bad" by itself — the goal is to bring it down to the level the
business genuinely needs and to replace expensive short-term debt with cheaper or
more appropriate funding.

> **Note:** This is a general advisory checklist, not formal financial, tax, or
> legal advice. Validate specifics with your CFO/CA and lenders against audited
> numbers and sanction terms.

## Step 0 — Diagnose before you act

The right fix depends on *why* the CC is at ₹50 cr. Pull these numbers first:

- **Utilisation:** Is the ₹50 cr fully drawn vs. sanctioned limit and drawing power?
- **Working-capital cycle:** Days Sales Outstanding (DSO), Days Inventory
  Outstanding (DIO), Days Payable Outstanding (DPO).
  - Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) = DSO + DIO − DPO.
- **End use:** Is the CC funding genuine current-asset growth, or plugging losses,
  capex, or long-term assets? (A CC funding fixed assets is a *mismatch* and the
  single most common reason a line balloons and never comes down.)
- **Cost:** Current interest rate on CC vs. alternative instruments.

If the CC is funding losses or fixed assets, no amount of working-capital tweaking
fixes it — you need equity or a term loan (see Lever C).

## Lever A — Shrink the working-capital cycle (the core fix)

Every day you cut from the cash conversion cycle releases cash that pays down CC.

**Receivables (lower DSO):**
- Tighten credit policy; enforce credit limits and stop-supply rules.
- Incentivise early payment (e.g. 1–2% discount for payment within 10 days).
- Systematic collections: ageing reviews, dunning, dedicated collections owner.
- **Bill discounting / factoring / TReDS** to convert receivables to cash without
  drawing on CC (TReDS is well-suited for MSME buyer invoices in India).

**Inventory (lower DIO):**
- Demand-based planning; reduce safety stock on fast-moving SKUs.
- Liquidate slow-moving / dead / obsolete stock, even at a discount — it frees cash
  and reduces drawing-power inflation.
- Vendor-managed inventory (VMI) / consignment stock where possible.
- ABC analysis: tight control on high-value 'A' items.

**Payables (raise DPO, prudently):**
- Renegotiate supplier terms (e.g. 30 → 60 days) without losing early-pay discounts
  that are cheaper than CC interest.
- **Supply-chain / vendor finance** so suppliers get paid early by a financier while
  you pay later — moves financing off your CC.

## Lever B — Replace CC with cheaper / better-structured debt

CC is usually among the most expensive short-term debt. Swap portions of it:

- **Working Capital Demand Loan (WCDL):** Carve out the *permanent* (never-zero)
  portion of the CC into a WCDL at a lower rate. The CC then handles only the
  fluctuating peak.
- **Commercial Paper (CP):** If well-rated, CP is materially cheaper than CC for the
  stable core requirement.
- **Trade finance instead of cash drawdowns:** Letters of Credit, buyer's/supplier's
  credit, LC-backed bill discounting for purchases.
- **Export finance (if you export):** Packing Credit in Foreign Currency (PCFC) /
  pre- and post-shipment credit at concessional rates.
- **Channel / dealer financing** so distributors' purchases are financed by a bank
  rather than sitting in your receivables and your CC.

These don't reduce total borrowing on day one, but they cut interest cost and
right-size the CC line, which is the lasting reduction.

## Lever C — Bring in long-term funds (deleverage)

If the CC is structurally too high (funding capex/losses or chronic over-reliance):

- **Promoter / equity infusion** or rights issue — the cleanest reduction.
- **Internal accruals:** Ring-fence retained profits to systematically pay down CC.
- **Refinance the mismatch:** If CC has financed fixed assets, take a **term loan**
  to repay that chunk — matches long-term assets to long-term funding.
- **Monetise non-core assets:** Sell surplus land, idle equipment, non-core
  investments; **sale-and-leaseback** of owned property to release locked capital.

## Lever D — Operational & treasury discipline

- **Cash-flow forecasting** (13-week rolling) so you draw on CC only when truly needed
  and sweep idle balances back against it.
- **Auto-sweep / cash-credit sweep accounts** so surplus current-account balances
  automatically reduce the CC outstanding and save interest daily.
- **Centralise treasury** across group entities to net internal balances.
- **Improve margins / cut costs** — every rupee of extra profit can deleverage.

## Lever E — Engage the bank proactively

- Negotiate a **lower spread** on the CC given improved ratios / credit rating.
- Restructure the limit: split into CC + WCDL, or reduce the sanctioned limit (lower
  commitment/unused-limit charges) once the cycle improves.
- Discuss **interest rate reset** benchmarks and review timing.

## Suggested 90-day action plan

| Priority | Action | Owner | Cash impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compute CCC; identify root cause (WC growth vs. mismatch vs. losses) | CFO | Diagnostic |
| 2 | Collections drive on overdue receivables + start bill discounting/TReDS | Finance/Sales | High, fast |
| 3 | Liquidate dead/slow inventory; tighten reorder levels | Ops/SCM | Medium, fast |
| 4 | Carve permanent CC core into WCDL/CP at lower rate | CFO + Bank | Cost saving |
| 5 | Renegotiate supplier terms + roll out vendor finance | Procurement | Medium |
| 6 | Decide equity infusion / term-loan refinance for any asset mismatch | Promoters/Board | High, structural |
| 7 | Set up auto-sweep + 13-week cash forecast | Treasury | Ongoing |

## Quick prioritisation logic

- **Need fast, low-risk reduction:** Lever A (collections + inventory) + auto-sweep.
- **CC too expensive but business is healthy:** Lever B (WCDL/CP/trade finance).
- **CC is structurally too high (funded assets/losses):** Lever C (equity/term loan)
  — working-capital tweaks alone won't fix it.

To tailor these numbers, the key inputs are: current DSO/DIO/DPO, CC interest rate,
sanctioned limit vs. drawing power, and what the CC has actually funded.
